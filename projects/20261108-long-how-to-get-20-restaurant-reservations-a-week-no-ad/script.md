# How to Get 20 Restaurant Reservations a Week — No Ads

## Script

### 01 · HOOK
You want 20 extra restaurant reservations a week without paying for ads. Here are five low-cost steps you can do this week that focus on local people, not clicks, and copyable scripts you can use right now.
*Doodle:* small restaurant with 5 tables, one clock · *On screen:* 20 Reservations, No Ads

### 02 · STAKES
If you don’t fill seats, revenue drops fast. A steady twenty reservations a week adds stable midday or weekend covers and makes staffing predictable. That stability keeps quality up and stress down for owners and staff.
*Footage:* empty restaurant dining room · *On screen:* Empty Tables Cost Money

### 03 · STAKES
You won’t need fancy photography or a big budget. You need simple systems: local outreach, an easy offer, and follow-up. Small restaurants, bakeries, and salons can run these with one person over a few hours a week.
*Footage:* owner writing list in cafe · *On screen:* Simple Systems Only

### 04 · STEP 1
Step one: create a limited-time, low-friction offer. For a restaurant: a weekdays two-course prix fixe for $18 with a seat guarantee. For a bakery: morning pastry combo. For a nail salon: discount add-on. Keep the offer clear and short.
*Doodle:* menu board leaning against wall, plate next to it · *On screen:* Weekday Prix Fixe Offer

### 05 · STEP 1
Example wording to copy: 'Weekday Two-Course Deal — $18. Book Mon–Thu 4–6 PM. Limited seats. Call or reserve online.' Put the price and days in the headline. Price reassures people and speeds decisions.
*Footage:* server handing menu to couple · *On screen:* Copy This Headline

### 06 · STEP 1
Cost and setup: nothing if you use existing menu items. If you want to promote a special pastry box, buy simple printed tent cards — $10 for 25. Put them on each table and at the host stand.
*Doodle:* stack of tent cards, hand placing one on table · *On screen:* $10 Table Cards

### 07 · STEP 1
Restaurant example: announce the offer in one sentence on your phone reservation script and on the website homepage. Train hosts: say the offer at confirmation. That single sentence increases opt-ins from callers and walk-ins.
*Footage:* host answering phone at restaurant · *On screen:* Train Hosts To Mention

### 08 · STEP 1
Bonus tweak: create a short booking code like 'WEEKDAY18' and require it to access the deal. That helps you track which channel works and prevents misuse. Use your reservation notes to store the code.
*Doodle:* computer with small calendar, cursor pointing · *On screen:* Use Booking Code

### 09 · STEP 2
Step two: reach nearby customers where they already are. Start with a 1,000-foot radius: neighbors, office buildings, and parking lots. You’re looking for people who can walk in or decide quickly to show up.
*Doodle:* map pin with circle around restaurant · *On screen:* Target Local Radius

### 10 · STEP 2
Example tactic: door drop postcards to 500 nearby homes. Print doublesided 4x6 cards for about $30 online. Headline, offer line, call or booking code on the card. Drop them in mailboxes or hand them to people leaving offices.
*Footage:* person dropping mail at doorstep · *On screen:* $30 For 500 Cards

### 11 · STEP 2
Script to use on the card: 'Local Dinner Deal — Two courses $18, Mon–Thu 4–6 PM. Call to reserve: (area code) 555-0100. Bring this card for faster seating.' Keep phone prominent and big.
*Doodle:* hand holding a small postcard, house door · *On screen:* Clear Phone On Card

### 12 · STEP 2
If you can’t do drop cards, partner with a neighboring bakery or cafe. Offer a shared flyer on their counter and give them a reciprocal shoutout online. Trade is often free and reaches people already out buying food.
*Footage:* baker handing customer box · *On screen:* Counter Flyer Swap

### 13 · STEP 2
Low-cost alternative: text neighbors using a printed sign in windows asking people to text a number to get the deal. Use a free texting tool trial. Make the keyword short and the reply immediate with reservation steps.
*Doodle:* phone with speech bubble and map pin · *On screen:* Text For Deal

### 14 · STEP 3
Step three: use your reservation flow to convert curiosity into bookings. Every call, walk-in, or message should have a script. Train staff to ask one question that moves people to book instead of answer later.
*Footage:* manager training staff at table · *On screen:* Script Your Booking Flow

### 15 · STEP 3
Script to copy: 'We have that $18 two-course slot Mon–Thu at 5:30. Can I hold two seats under your name? A quick phone number will hold it for 15 minutes.' That urgency prompts immediate action.
*Doodle:* host with pen and small open book · *On screen:* Copy This Phone Script

### 16 · STEP 3
Example for a bakery: when someone asks about morning buns, say: 'We reserve combo boxes for pickup until noon — can I hold one under your name for 30 minutes?' A small window increases conversions noticeably.
*Footage:* baker handing customer box · *On screen:* Hold A Combo Box

### 17 · STEP 3
Add reservations to your foot traffic routine: ask every dine-in guest if they want to reserve for next week. Offer the deal as a reason to come back. Train servers to mention it at check delivery or when paying.
*Doodle:* server leaning over table with small hand gesture · *On screen:* Ask Every Guest

### 18 · STEP 3
Cost and tools: a free shared Google Sheet or your booking app notes are enough to track reservations and codes. Mark which reservations came from local cards, text, or host mentions to double down on what works.
*Footage:* owner updating spreadsheet on laptop · *On screen:* Track Source Simply

### 19 · STEP 4
Step four: create a short referral loop. Offer a low-priced reward for guests who bring friends during slow nights. For restaurants it can be a free dessert per two adults. For a plumber, a small courtesy checkup for referrals.
*Doodle:* two people handing dessert plate to another · *On screen:* Referral Reward

### 20 · STEP 4
Referral wording on receipt or card: 'Refer a friend and both get a free dessert when you book the Weekday Deal. Mention your name when reserving.' It’s cheap and feels like a thank-you rather than a discount.
*Footage:* cashier handing receipt to customer · *On screen:* Refer & Get Dessert

### 21 · STEP 4
Nail salon example: give a free polish upgrade to both the referrer and the friend when they book a weekday slot. Price it into your margin — small freebies create word-of-mouth that pays off on repeat visits.
*Doodle:* manicure hand and small polish bottle · *On screen:* Free Polish Upgrade

### 22 · STEP 4
Promote referrals on the table cards and receipts. Train hosts to mention 'bring a friend' when confirming reservations. Make the reward immediate so people see value when planning a night out with friends.
*Footage:* host handing card to couple · *On screen:* Promote On Receipt

### 23 · STEP 4
Measure success by counting bookings that use the referral phrase or show the card. If referrals account for a few bookings a week, scale by adding a larger visible sign or small social posts calling out the deal.
*Doodle:* clipboard with checkmarks, pen · *On screen:* Count Referral Bookings

### 24 · STEP 5
Step five: use immediate follow-up to lock no-shows and turn callers into guests. Send a confirmation text the same day with the reservation time and a one-sentence reminder of the deal to reduce cancellations.
*Footage:* host sending text message · *On screen:* Confirm With Text

### 25 · STEP 5
Text template to copy: 'Thanks — we’ve held two seats for you on Tue at 5:30 for Weekday Deal $18. Reply YES to confirm or CALL to hold longer.' Short, actionable, and phone-first for easy replies.
*Doodle:* phone with speech bubble and small checkmark · *On screen:* Copy This Text

### 26 · STEP 5
If you use email, keep it under two lines and include a map pin or easy directions. For a bakery pickup, confirm the pickup window and offer to hold the box for 30 minutes after arrival to remove friction.
*Footage:* barista handing pastry to customer · *On screen:* Short Email Confirm

### 27 · STEP 5
Use a cheap no-show protection: note repeat offenders and ask for a small deposit for large weekend bookings. Make this a last step, not the first. Most local guests will appreciate the clarity and convenience.
*Doodle:* owner pointing to small notebook labeled 'notes' · *On screen:* No-show Note System

### 28 · STEP 5
Restaurant example: follow up a day before with the reservation text and a single menu highlight like 'Chef’s soup included.' Small reminders increase show rates and make diners feel anticipated, which reduces last-minute cancellations.
*Footage:* chef stirring soup pot · *On screen:* One-Day Reminder

### 29 · MISTAKE
Common mistake: you spread yourself thin over every channel and never measure what works. If you try ten tactics at once you won’t know the winners. Pick two channels, run them for three weeks, and track bookings by source.
*Doodle:* person juggling many small icons like mail and phones · *On screen:* Don’t Spread Too Thin

### 30 · MISTAKE
Another mistake: confusing a deal with brand damage. If your offer feels like desperation, keep the presentation professional, price it reasonably, and limit availability. A tidy offer increases perceived value rather than cheapening the brand.
*Footage:* server arranging table neatly · *On screen:* Keep Offers Professional

### 31 · RECAP
Quick recap. One, design a clear limited-time offer. Two, reach nearby people with low-cost touchpoints. Three, script your reservation flow. Four, reward referrals. Five, confirm and follow up. Each step feeds the next to create steady bookings.
*Doodle:* five numbered icons in a row, small chef hat · *On screen:* Five Steps Recap

### 32 · RECAP
Concrete first actions this week: print 100 table cards, drop 500 cards nearby, train hosts on the booking script, set up a referral card, and send a confirmation text. These five tasks take a few hours and little cash.
*Footage:* staff placing table cards · *On screen:* Five Tasks This Week

### 33 · RECAP
If you run these steps and track where bookings come from, you can tweak the highest-performing touchpoints. Double down on what brings seats and cut what doesn’t. That’s how you scale to twenty reservations a week without paid ads.
*Doodle:* clipboard with two big checkmarks · *On screen:* Double Down On Winners

### 34 · RECAP
Remember: these are tactical, low-cost moves, not overnight magic. Use concrete wording, small visible offers, and follow-up. Even a tiny increase in weekly reservations compounds into predictable revenue over months.
*Footage:* couple smiling in restaurant · *On screen:* Small Moves, Big Impact

### 35 · RECAP
Final tip: keep offers short, measurable, and time-limited. Price should feel like value. Track the booking code or phrase to know what worked, then repeat the steps that bring people through your door.
