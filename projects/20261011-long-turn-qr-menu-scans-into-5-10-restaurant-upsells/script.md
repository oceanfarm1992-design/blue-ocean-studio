# Turn QR Menu Scans into $5–$10 Restaurant Upsells

## Script

### 01 · HOOK
Every QR menu scan is an attention moment. Convert just one in ten scans into a $5 add-on and a 50-seat restaurant does hundreds of extra dollars per night. You can do this without new software or big budgets.
*Doodle:* hand holding phone scanning a round QR sticker on a table · *On screen:* Make QR scans pay

### 02 · STAKES
If you ignore upsells on QR menus you leave easy revenue on the table. Guests already decide on their phones at the table. If you don’t suggest a small extra they rarely add it. That’s lost $5–$10 per guest, repeatedly.
*Footage:* diner scanning QR at table · *On screen:* Stop leaving money

### 03 · STEP 1 - EXPLAIN THE IDEA
Step one: treat every QR scan like a micro-opportunity. Add one clear, low-friction offer at the top of your menu page that guests can add with one tap. Keep it cheap, useful, and relevant to the dish they already picked.
*Doodle:* phone with a big button labeled as an add-on (no text shown), plate beside phone · *On screen:* One-tap add-on

### 04 · STEP 1 - HOW TO IMPLEMENT
Use your existing QR menu provider or a web page editor. Add a small banner above the menu that links to a quick add-to-order form or a server note. You can do this in minutes and for free on many platforms.
*Footage:* server typing on laptop in restaurant · *On screen:* Add banner above menu

### 05 · STEP 1 - WORDING TO COPY
Write one sentence that sells the add-on. For example: “Add our warm garlic bread for $5 — ready in 2 minutes.” Keep the line short, benefit-led, and time-limited when possible to push instant adds.
*Doodle:* small plate of bread with an arrow pointing to it, clock icon nearby · *On screen:* Add garlic bread $5

### 06 · STEP 1 - BAKERY EXAMPLE
Bakery: put “Add a warm butter croissant for $3 — baked fresh now” at the top. Hook dessert or coffee combos near checkout. Cost to you is under $0.50 per item. Price it to feel like a bargain for guests.
*Footage:* baker handing customer croissant · *On screen:* Warm croissant $3

### 07 · STEP 1 - PLUMBER EXAMPLE
Plumber with a stall or community event: include a small offer when people sign in: “Add pipe-insurance check for $10.” Make it quick and valuable for the customer. It’s a mental add-on, not an invasive upsell.
*Footage:* plumber at community stall signing clipboard · *On screen:* Pipe check $10

### 08 · STEP 1 - NAIL SALON EXAMPLE
Nail salon: on the QR booking menu add “Add a 5-minute hand massage for $5.” It’s short, cheap, and complements main service. Train techs to note it when they see it on the tablet so it’s delivered immediately.
*Footage:* manicurist prepping tools at station · *On screen:* Hand massage $5

### 09 · STEP 2 - CHOOSE NATIVE FITS
Step two: pick offers that naturally match existing menu items. Think like a guest. If they order a steak, suggest a $6 garlic butter or a side salad. If coffee, suggest a pastry. Keep price small, perceived value high.
*Doodle:* three plates with arrows pointing to small add-on plates · *On screen:* Match the main item

### 10 · STEP 2 - HOW TO IMPLEMENT
Map your top five menu items. Give each a single relevant add-on priced between $3 and $10. Add the suggestion into the QR menu near each item and in the cart stage. One suggestion per item keeps choices simple.
*Footage:* chef writing menu on notepad · *On screen:* Map top five items

### 11 · STEP 2 - WORDING TO COPY
Use three words of benefit plus price. Example: “Creamy garlic butter — $6,” or “Extra crunch topping — $4.” Guests skim; short, clear benefits convert best. Avoid long descriptions that dilute the instant add decision.
*Doodle:* small butter pat on a spoon next to a plate · *On screen:* Creamy garlic butter $6

### 12 · STEP 2 - BAKERY EXAMPLE
Bakery: pair any coffee with “Fresh muffin — $3” shown next to the coffee listing. Make it one tap to add at the cart. Show a small image so the brain fills in taste quickly and adds without thinking.
*Footage:* barista placing muffin next to coffee cup · *On screen:* Fresh muffin $3

### 13 · STEP 2 - PLUMBER EXAMPLE
Plumber: when someone books a call, offer “Priority scheduling bump — $10.” It’s a logical add for busy customers. Offer a checkbox on your booking form or tell the scheduler to confirm it verbally on calls.
*Footage:* plumber talking on phone in van with clipboard · *On screen:* Priority $10

### 14 · STEP 2 - NAIL SALON EXAMPLE
Nail salon: on the QR service list add “Quick polish refresh — $5” under manicure choices. It’s an impulse that fits timing and perceived value. Train staff to offer it once when they greet the guest.
*Footage:* customer choosing nail color from swatches · *On screen:* Polish refresh $5

### 15 · STEP 3 - MAKE ADDING FRICTIONLESS
Step three: minimize taps. Guests abandon carts if it’s clunky. Use your menu tool to let customers add the item with one tap, or make a clear note that servers will add it if selected. Fewer clicks equals higher conversion.
*Doodle:* hand with one finger pressing a large tablet button · *On screen:* One tap to add

### 16 · STEP 3 - HOW TO IMPLEMENT
If your menu platform can’t do one-tap adds, use a simple form button that writes an order note. Alternatively print a small QR sticker for add-ons that opens a single-item checkout page for quick payment and instant kitchen notice.
*Footage:* owner typing on tablet behind counter · *On screen:* Single checkout page

### 17 · STEP 3 - WORDING TO COPY
On the button use action language: “Add Garlic Butter — $6” or “Yes, add muffin $3.” Don’t be coy. Explicit price and action reduce friction and increase trust. Guests click when they know exactly what they pay for.
*Doodle:* large rounded button being pressed by a finger · *On screen:* Yes, add it

### 18 · STEP 3 - BAKERY EXAMPLE
Bakery: a QR add-ons sticker on each table opens a one-item checkout for “Warm croissant $3.” Payment takes 30 seconds. Staff sees the order as a flagged item and heats it immediately. Set staff roles for fast delivery.
*Footage:* customer picking up croissant from display · *On screen:* One-tap croissant

### 19 · STEP 3 - PLUMBER EXAMPLE
Plumber: when onsite, leave a small QR card that offers a quick minor service like filter swap for $10. Customer scans, pays, and you perform it. That avoids awkward sales talks and closes while trust is high.
*Footage:* plumber holding small service card at kitchen sink · *On screen:* Filter swap $10

### 20 · STEP 3 - NAIL SALON EXAMPLE
Nail salon: on the booking tablet use a checkbox labeled “Add 5-min hand massage $5.” Have the front desk verify it at check-in. This reduces forgetfulness and makes it a seamless bolt-on during the session.
*Footage:* receptionist checking appointment on tablet · *On screen:* Add hand massage

### 21 · STEP 4 - PRICE AND MARGIN MATH
Step four: price to win. Aim for $5–$10 retail with a low cost to you. A $5 add-on that costs you $1 nets useful margin. Keep costs under 30 percent of retail where possible so the upsell is profitable for the business.
*Doodle:* calculator next to a coin stack and dollar symbol · *On screen:* Price to win

### 22 · STEP 4 - HOW TO IMPLEMENT
Calculate food or labor cost per add-on. If a side costs $1.20 to make, sell for $5. If a five-minute massage costs $2 labor, sell $5. Small ticket prices make the decision easy for guests and keep margins healthy.
*Footage:* chef checking orders and cost sheet · *On screen:* Know your cost

### 23 · STEP 4 - WORDING TO COPY
Frame price as a small extra. Use phrases like “Add for just $5” or “Only $6 more.” The word “just” or “only” reduces perceived pain. Be honest about what they get so trust stays high and repeat customers aren’t lost.
*Doodle:* small smiling face next to a simple price tag icon · *On screen:* Only $5 more

### 24 · STEP 4 - BAKERY EXAMPLE
Bakery: cost for a warmed croissant might be $0.40. Selling at $3 leaves strong margin and a perceived treat. Train staff to mention “just $3” when guests ask recommendations. Low price equals high attach rate.
*Footage:* bakery counter with croissants in display · *On screen:* Cost $0.40, Sell $3

### 25 · STEP 4 - PLUMBER EXAMPLE
Plumber: a small filter swap add-on costing you under $5 can retail at $15. The perceived value of convenience is high. Include a short line in your QR booking confirmation so customers can add it before you arrive.
*Footage:* plumber holding filter beside sink · *On screen:* Swap filter $15

### 26 · STEP 4 - NAIL SALON EXAMPLE
Nail salon: a 5-minute hand massage has low labor and no material cost. Price it $5–$8. It boosts revenue per chair and takes almost no time. Include a short checkbox on booking so clients add it in advance.
*Footage:* manicurist giving hand massage · *On screen:* Massage $5–$8

### 27 · STEP 5 - TRAIN STAFF AND CONFIRM DELIVERY
Step five: train staff to acknowledge and fulfill add-ons. If a guest taps to add, staff must confirm it verbally and deliver promptly. Failure to deliver kills repeat buys. Make fulfillment simple and assign clear roles for each add-on type.
*Doodle:* one server with checklist nodding and a clock icon · *On screen:* Confirm and deliver

### 28 · STEP 5 - HOW TO IMPLEMENT
Run a 15-minute staff huddle. Show them the new banner and the exact phrases to use. Assign one person to monitor add-on orders on the POS and one to prepare them. Practice the handoff once during a slow shift.
*Footage:* restaurant staff huddle in kitchen · *On screen:* 15-minute training

### 29 · STEP 5 - WORDING TO COPY
Script for staff: “Great choice — would you like our warm garlic bread for just $5?” If the guest already added it, staff should say, “Perfect — we’ll bring that right away.” Short scripts reduce hesitation and improve delivery.
*Doodle:* server leaning slightly toward guest with speech line indicated · *On screen:* Would you like garlic bread?

### 30 · STEP 5 - BAKERY EXAMPLE
Bakery: the barista confirms an add-on by saying, “Thanks — we’ll warm that and drop it with your coffee.” Label the warming tray with table numbers so delivery is fast. Speed reinforces the impulse purchase was a good idea.
*Footage:* barista placing warmed pastry on tray · *On screen:* Drop with coffee

### 31 · STEP 5 - PLUMBER EXAMPLE
Plumber: when an on-site QR add is chosen have the technician confirm, “I’ll do the filter swap while I’m here.” Add it to the work order board so billing is clear. Clear confirmation removes awkwardness and increases acceptance.
*Footage:* technician writing on work order clipboard · *On screen:* Confirmed on work order

### 32 · STEP 5 - NAIL SALON EXAMPLE
Nail salon: the tech confirms at chair: “I see you added the hand massage — I’ll do that first so your polish isn’t disturbed.” That clear sequence reassures clients and ensures the add-on is performed well.
*Footage:* technician preparing client hand at nail station · *On screen:* Massage confirmed

### 33 · STEP 5 - MEASURE RESULTS
Track add-on attach rate and fulfillment time for one week. Example target: 5–15 percent attach rate depending on price and foot traffic. If it’s lower, tweak wording, placement, or staff script and test again the next week.
*Doodle:* bar chart with one highlighted bar and upward arrow · *On screen:* Track attach rate

### 34 · MISTAKE
Big mistake: giving too many choices or hiding the price. Too many options paralyze guests. Hidden costs destroy trust. Keep one clear add-on per decision point, show price upfront, and make it simple to accept or skip.
*Doodle:* single person surrounded by many arrows pointing to small icons · *On screen:* Don’t confuse guests

### 35 · RECAP
Recap: one clear add-on up top, natural pairings, one-tap adds, fair pricing, and staff confirmation. Implement for five top items, train staff for 15 minutes, measure a week, then iterate. Small changes can create reliable $5–$10 increases.
*Doodle:* simple checklist with five checked boxes and a checkmark · *On screen:* One clear add-on
