## Script

### 01 · HOOK
You run a cafe. You’re short on staff and time. Posting nothing on Instagram costs you repeat customers you never met. This plan gets you four ready-to-post Reels in one focused hour, so you keep your feed fresh without burning your team out.
*Footage:* barista closing laptop · *On screen:* 4 Reels • 1 Hour

### 02 · HOOK
You don’t need a film crew. You need a plan and the right shots. I’ll give you four simple Reel formats, exact lines to say, and the order to film them so you finish in an hour and have a week of content.
*Footage:* busy cafe counter · *On screen:* No crew needed

### 03 · STAKES
If you skip content for weeks, regulars forget you. People discover cafes on social first, not Google or newspapers. Every week you publish, you increase the chance someone walks in this weekend. Consistency matters more than perfection.
*Footage:* empty cafe tables · *On screen:* Consistency beats perfection

### 04 · STEP 1 - REEL #1: BEHIND-THE-SCENES
Reel one is a 15 to 30 second behind-the-scenes. Show how you make a signature item. Example: the bakery-cafe — film pulling a baguette from the oven, sprinkling flour, and slicing. Say: 'Fresh all morning. Come grab this warm loaf.'
*Footage:* baker pulling bread oven · *On screen:* Behind-the-scenes

### 05 · STEP 1 - REEL #1: BEHIND-THE-SCENES
Shots: 3 to 5 clips — oven pull, close-up crust, cutting, plated loaf. Keep clips 2 to 4 seconds each. Sound: natural oven ambient and one line of voiceover recorded on your phone. Cost: zero. Tip: film during slow prep, not rush hour.
*Footage:* close-up slicing bread · *On screen:* 3–5 short clips

### 06 · STEP 1 - REEL #1: EXAMPLE
Script to copy: 'This is our sourdough. Baked at 5am, hand-shaped, slow fermented for texture. Grab one warm while it lasts.' That’s 10 to 12 seconds of speaking. Keep it honest and specific to build trust.
*Footage:* hands tearing bread · *On screen:* Say this line

### 07 · STEP 2 - REEL #2: QUICK MENU HIGHLIGHT
Reel two focuses on a menu item that drives visits — like your weekend croissant or lunchtime sandwich. Show the finished item, the price, and one quick benefit: 'Buttery layers, $3.50, great with Americano.' Short, specific, and makes it easy to buy.
*Footage:* barista pouring coffee · *On screen:* Menu Highlight

### 08 · STEP 2 - REEL #2: SHOTS & ORDER
Film in this order: item close-up, barista preparing the drink or wrap, price card on table, customer taking a bite. Use 3-second clips. Add text overlay with price and offer. Cost: free. You already have the product, so it’s pure content.
*Footage:* sandwich on plate · *On screen:* Show price

### 09 · STEP 2 - REEL #2: EXAMPLE
Script to copy: 'New lunch special: roasted veggie wrap, $7.95. Ready in five minutes. Pair with a small cold brew for $2 extra.' Keep your tone like you’re talking to a regular. Clear price increases trust and reduces friction.
*Footage:* customer bite sandwich · *On screen:* Say this line

### 10 · STEP 3 - REEL #3: STAFF PERSONALITY
Reel three is personality. Let one staff member answer a fun question on camera. Example question: 'What’s your secret coffee hack?' Keep answers 8 to 12 seconds. Customers connect with faces and repeat visits follow when they feel a relationship.
*Footage:* barista smiling camera · *On screen:* Staff Personality

### 11 · STEP 3 - REEL #3: SHOTS & TIPS
Film a mid-shot of the team member, a close-up of their hands doing the hack, and a reaction shot. Use natural light by the window. Tip: if someone is camera shy, let them read a line you wrote. Offer a free pastry for participating.
*Footage:* barista frothing milk · *On screen:* 3 quick shots

### 12 · STEP 3 - REEL #3: EXAMPLE
Script to copy: 'I add a pinch of cinnamon to the milk before steaming. It makes warm notes pop.' Then show the pinch and the steam. Short, authentic, repeatable. Customers will try it and tag you when they do.
*Footage:* hand sprinkling cinnamon · *On screen:* Say this line

### 13 · STEP 4 - REEL #4: CUSTOMER MOMENT
Reel four is social proof. Capture a real customer enjoying their drink and ask for permission to film. Show their smile, the cup, and a one-sentence reason they picked the cafe. Authentic testimonials beat scripted promos online.
*Footage:* customer sipping coffee · *On screen:* Customer Moment

### 14 · STEP 4 - REEL #4: HOW TO FILM FAST
Approach during checkout: offer a free small cookie if they let you film 10 seconds. Ask: 'What made you stop by today?' Film their answer, then a close-up of their cup. Keep consent quick and friendly. Cost: a cookie or sample.
*Footage:* barista handing cookie · *On screen:* Offer a small cookie

### 15 · STEP 4 - REEL #4: EXAMPLE
Verbatim question and prompt to copy: 'Can I film a quick 10-second clip? We’ll give you a free cookie.' If they agree, say: 'What made you stop in today?' Let them answer in their own words. Keep it short and natural.
*Footage:* customer nodding yes · *On screen:* Ask this question

### 16 · MISTAKE
Common mistake: trying to make each Reel a commercial. People scroll quickly. If your Reel feels like an ad, it gets skipped. Focus on curiosity, value, and real moments. Two authentic seconds are better than ten polished but boring seconds.
*Footage:* phone showing bored viewer · *On screen:* Don’t be an ad

### 17 · MISTAKE
Another mistake is over-editing. Keep cuts simple. Use the in-app trim and a single music track. Spend the saved time connecting with customers instead. Too many effects make your cafe look like a different business.
*Footage:* person editing phone app · *On screen:* Edit lightly

### 18 · MISTAKE
Also avoid perfect scripts. Give staff one line to say and let them be themselves. Customers buy from people, not polished lines. If a barista laughs or stumbles, keep it—authenticity performs better on Reels.
*Footage:* barista laughing camera · *On screen:* Keep it real

### 19 · RECAP
Quick recap: Reel one — behind-the-scenes baking. Reel two — menu highlight with price. Reel three — staff personality hack. Reel four — short customer moment. Film in this order while cleanups or prep happen and you can finish in an hour.
*Footage:* three staff prepping · *On screen:* Weekly Reel System

### 20 · RECAP
Timing: aim for three takes per clip and move on. Total filming time: 45 to 60 minutes. Editing in the Instagram app should take another 10 to 20 minutes. Publish one Reel per day or spread across the week. Consistency wins.
*Footage:* clock on wall cafe · *On screen:* 45–60 minutes

### 21 · RECAP
Posting notes: use 3 to 5 relevant hashtags, a short caption with location, and pin one comment offering a small incentive — like 'Show this Reel for a free cookie today.' Incentives increase walk-ins with measurable results.
*Footage:* writing caption phone · *On screen:* Pin an incentive

### 22 · RECAP
Reuse clips across platforms. Crop vertical Reels into stories, or upload the same short video to Facebook. You get more reach for the same effort. Don’t repost the same clip every day—space them out to avoid follower fatigue.
*Footage:* owner posting phone · *On screen:* Reuse content

### 23 · ADDITIONAL TIP
Low-cost boosting: if a Reel performs well organically, boost it with a small daily budget of $5 to $10 to reach more local people. Target a 5-kilometer radius and weekdays around your busiest hours. Test one Reel at a time.
*Footage:* owner tapping promote · *On screen:* Boost winning Reels

### 24 · ADDITIONAL TIP
Track simple metrics: saves, shares, and comments matter more than likes. If people save or share your Reel, it means they plan to act. Keep a notebook and note weekly which Reel got the best saves to repeat that format.
*Footage:* notebook on counter · *On screen:* Track saves & shares

### 25 · EXTRA EXAMPLE
Example week for a small espresso bar: Monday behind-the-scenes shot of latte art, Tuesday menu highlight on a pastry, Wednesday staff tip on grind size, Friday customer tasting reaction. This schedule keeps different angles and audience interest.
*Footage:* latte art close-up · *On screen:* Example week

### 26 · EXTRA EXAMPLE
For a lunch cafe: film midday prep for the menu highlight, a quick staff tip on reheating soups, and customer clips at peak lunch. For a bakery, focus more on ovens and fresh pulls because scent and freshness sell pastries.
*Footage:* chef stirring soup · *On screen:* Tailor to niche

### 27 · FINAL NOTE
Remember: the goal is consistency, not cinematic perfection. Four short Reels a week keeps your cafe top of mind. Use natural light, short clips, and simple captions. Over time you’ll find which formats drive the most foot traffic.
*Footage:* sunlit cafe window · *On screen:* Consistency > Perfection
