## Script

### 01 · HOOK
You check your Google listing and you see lots of views. But the phone stays silent. If your listing looks busy but doesn't bring jobs, you're losing money. This video explains the common reasons and simple fixes that cost little or nothing.
*Footage:* person checking phone · *On screen:* Views, No Calls?

### 02 · HOOK
Imagine a plumber with 500 views a month and not a single call. That happens when people see you but don’t know what to do next. You can fix this without a big ad budget or a new website.
*Footage:* plumber at van · *On screen:* Fix it cheap

### 03 · STAKES
Every view is a potential lead. If even a small percent convert, you get jobs and revenue. If none do, that traffic is wasted. You need the listing to answer questions and remove friction so people feel safe to call.
*Footage:* money slipping away · *On screen:* Traffic = Potential

### 04 · STAKES
You also compete with big chains and booking apps. They might get the calls because they make it obvious how to hire them. You can beat them with clear copy, photos, and tiny trust signals.
*Footage:* small business vs chain · *On screen:* Stand out

### 05 · STEP 1
Step 1: Make the call-to-action impossible to miss. Replace vague 'Contact' buttons with 'Call Now for Same-Day Service' or 'Book Emergency Leak Repair.' On your Google profile title and short description repeat the CTA once.
*Footage:* typing call to action · *On screen:* Clear CTA

### 06 · STEP 1
Example: Plumber wording to copy: 'Call now for same-day leak repairs — licensed plumber, free quote over phone.' Put that as the first line in the business description. People scan quickly; lead with the ask.
*Footage:* plumber phone call · *On screen:* Copy this text

### 07 · STEP 2
Step 2: Make calling frictionless. Use a single visible phone number and a click-to-call button. If you use a tracking number, route calls to your main line. Test the number from different phones to make sure it rings.
*Footage:* phone number on screen · *On screen:* Easy to Call

### 08 · STEP 2
Example: Bakery routine. Use the same number on Google, your site, and Facebook. If you add a local tracking number, expect a small monthly fee — around ten dollars a month [CHECK: price]. Make sure it forwards to your main phone.
*Footage:* bakery storefront phone · *On screen:* Single Number

### 09 · STEP 3
Step 3: Use business hours and attributes to match intent. If you do emergency calls, mark '24/7' or 'On-site services.' If you don’t, don’t claim it. Honesty avoids angry customers and false clicks.
*Footage:* business hours sign · *On screen:* Set Hours Right

### 10 · STEP 3
Example: Nail salon with late slots. Add a 'Late appointments' attribute and show evening photos of the salon. Add the exact hours for late nights. People searching after work will click and call if they see availability.
*Footage:* nail salon evening · *On screen:* Show availability

### 11 · STEP 4
Step 4: Add photos that answer questions. Show your van, uniform, pricing board, receipts, and before-and-after shots. Customers call when they trust you look professional and local. Upload 5-10 clear photos and keep them updated.
*Footage:* business photos upload · *On screen:* Photos = Trust

### 12 · STEP 4
Example: Plumber photos to add: company van with logo, technician with ID, a clean invoice showing a flat service fee, and an after-repair photo. These reduce friction and answer 'is this legit?' before someone decides to call.
*Footage:* plumber photos collection · *On screen:* Add these photos

### 13 · STEP 5
Step 5: Use simple pricing cues. You don’t need full price lists, but give ranges. 'Drain cleaning from $79' or 'Service call fee $49' is enough to qualify callers. People avoid calling if they expect surprise fees.
*Footage:* price board closeup · *On screen:* Show Price Range

### 14 · STEP 5
Example: Bakery: 'Loaves from $3, birthday cakes starting $45.' Nail salon: 'Manicures from $25, walk-ins welcome.' This sets expectations and weeds out tire-kickers who just want prices over and over.
*Footage:* bakery price tags · *On screen:* Set expectations

### 15 · STEP 6
Step 6: Add social proof that people actually read. Post two recent reviews and reply to them on your listing. Complaints should get calm public replies offering a phone number to resolve. Positive replies boost trust.
*Footage:* replying to reviews · *On screen:* Show reviews

### 16 · STEP 6
Example: Plumber reply copy: 'Thanks, Maria. Glad the leak is fixed. Call us anytime 555-0000 for rapid service.' Nail salon reply copy: 'Thanks for coming in! Book by calling 555-0000. Ask about weekday discounts.' Use real phone number format.
*Footage:* handwriting thank you note · *On screen:* Reply publicly

### 17 · STEP 7
Step 7: Track calls so you can test changes. Use simple call tracking or just a spreadsheet. Note date, time, caller question, and whether it converted to a booking. Testing lets you see which tweaks actually increase calls.
*Footage:* notebook and phone · *On screen:* Track calls

### 18 · STEP 7
Example: Tracking idea for a plumber: add a line in your job log 'source: Google listing' and ask callers 'How did you find us?' Train staff to record the answer. That gives real data without extra cost.
*Footage:* plumber writing log · *On screen:* Ask callers

### 19 · MISTAKE
Common mistake: Listing looks great to you, but it doesn't answer the customer's single biggest question: will this solve my problem today? If you omit speed, price range, and proof, people will scroll to someone else.
*Footage:* user scrolling phone · *On screen:* Big Mistake

### 20 · MISTAKE
Another mistake: using stock photos or generic team pics. They don’t prove you’re local or real. Swap them for real shots of your shop, your van, and your receipts. That small swap increases trust and call rates.
*Footage:* stock photos on screen · *On screen:* Swap stock photos

### 21 · MISTAKE
Don’t hide your price or availability behind a 'Contact us' form. People calling expect the phone to be the fastest route. If you force them into a web form, many will abandon and call a competitor.
*Footage:* contact form on laptop · *On screen:* Avoid forms

### 22 · RECAP
Recap: Make your CTA obvious, use one phone number, show hours and attributes, add trust photos, give price cues, post reviews and reply, and track calls. Each fix costs little but removes friction that stops calls.
*Footage:* checklist recap · *On screen:* Quick Checklist

### 23 · RECAP
Quick example recap: For a plumber — clear 'Call Now for Same-Day Service' CTA, van and receipt photos, 'Service call $49' pricing, ask callers 'how did you find us?' and log each call. Small effort, better call volume.
*Footage:* plumber checklist · *On screen:* Plumber checklist

### 24 · RECAP
For a bakery — single phone number across profiles, price cues for common items, evening photos for event orders, and replies to reviews that include the phone number. People want confidence before they call.
*Footage:* bakery staff serving · *On screen:* Bakery tips

### 25 · RECAP
For a nail salon — show late hours, add 'walk-ins welcome' attribute if true, photo of a busy chair to signal demand, pricing for common services, and a clear click-to-call on mobile. Those small signals prompt calls.
*Footage:* nail salon busy · *On screen:* Salon tips

### 26 · CTA
If you fix these things and still get views without calls, run a simple A/B test: change the CTA wording for two weeks and track calls. Keep the version that brings more real bookings. Small tests beat guesswork.
